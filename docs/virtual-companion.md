# Little Omni

Little Omni is a native, lilac companion in the main chat and floating assistant. It has a soft body, two small ears and no legs. The artwork is drawn with Compose Canvas paths and gradients; the app does not load the documentation images at runtime.

![Artwork and placement illustration](companion-artwork.png)

The illustration shows the drawing and intended placement, not a device screenshot.

- Hops along the actual composer, visible agent consoles and the top edges of your message bubbles. Stable message/console keys let it ride a platform during scrolling. It jumps to another safe surface before its perch reaches a viewport edge, and escapes if a LazyColumn item disappears.
- Tap for a tumble and recovery; drag gently to choose a nearby perch, or fling it for a short gravity-driven flight with wall bounces. Placement, drag deltas and throw velocity use physical screen coordinates in both RTL and LTR layouts. Pickup freezes autonomous movement and tracks the finger in root coordinates. Landings compress and rebound; the ears sway with movement. It stays above the editor and within the transcript viewport.
- Eyes follow touches and the actual shaped text cursor while you type or move the selection. Arabic, English and mixed text use Compose paragraph geometry rather than a guessed writing direction. The editor owns its vertical text viewport so cursor positions include wrapping and draft scrolling. Eye direction follows the viewing angle instead of saturating at both extremes. After a typing pause it glances toward the visible keyboard area (not individual key touches, which belong to the IME), or the caret if no IME is visible; during work it watches the console.
- Expressions use actual run events for thinking, tools, confirmation, listening and errors. A short celebration follows a successful run; opening an old completed conversation or pressing Stop does not trigger one. Idle breathing, a small curious tilt and occasional ear twitch add subtle motion. It sleeps after idle time and wakes when you interact. Reduced motion disables these transforms.
- Long press opens a small inline controls card with a return-to-composer action and **Hide for 5 minutes**. This works in the floating assistant's existing window too. Outside touches dismiss it without consuming the underlying gesture; while open it pauses autonomous motion.
- Hiding starts a sad, slow sequence of small hops toward the nearest physical edge, clipped within the host. Tapping or grabbing the remaining visible part before it exits cancels departure: it looks happy, follows a held finger, then returns with three quick bounces. Other chat touches do not cancel departure. Reduced motion gives a stationary three-second sad grace period and an immediate happy rescue. Once hidden, its reserved gaps close smoothly; it returns after five minutes, on a new chat/host, or by toggling visibility back on. The preference stays enabled.
- Idle curiosity has varied intervals and avoids immediate repeats: looking around, sniffing, yawning, stretching and a small ear-grooming movement. Touch/typing, work, decision/listening states and reduced motion interrupt these moments. Its timer spans automatic hops so roaming cannot starve curiosity. Gentle petting gives a slow blink and a small lean; sleepy Omni settles onto a small cushion in its resting corner when roaming is allowed.
- Settings → Virtual companion controls visibility, automatic hopping, local learning, expressive moods, personality (Calm/Curious/Playful), and the physical resting corner (Learned/Left/Right). Preferences apply to both chat hosts. The memory reset clears learned habits and moods without removing the companion or changing these preferences.
- Reserves 60 dp above the composer/console and adds 36 dp above user bubbles, sharing the existing 24 dp transcript gap. It pauses when the host lifecycle stops, follows system reduced motion, and uses a 30 fps ticker on compact devices. Very short windows and assistant confirmation/tool panels hide it. Settings also offer a static expression preview.
- Only the 60 dp sprite and the open inline controls consume pointer input. The host observes touches for gaze and outside-dismissal without consuming scroll, selection or control gestures. Semantic actions support playing, opening controls, temporary hiding and returning to the composer. There are no extra windows, remote model calls or Activity result launchers.

## Local learning and memory

`CompanionLearning` implements a compact mobile adaptation of [EE-Net, JMLR 27(55), 2026](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v27/23-0582.html). One trainable MLP predicts action reward. A second MLP consumes a normalized signed sketch of the first network's full parameter gradient and learns the signed residual between observed reward and the **pre-update** prediction. Both networks use actual SGD updates with bounded gradients/weights. The implementation uses tanh networks and a fixed 24-dimensional sketch rather than the paper's LLE; its small-network, bounded-replay adaptation does not claim the paper's theoretical guarantees.

Learned scores choose among currently available safe actions. A cold-start contextual prior gradually gives way to learned scores over the first 24 meaningful observations. A short novelty history discourages immediate repetition. At most three stored experiences are replayed per new observation, alongside the new sample; 128 detailed experiences are retained, while weights and familiarity continue across sessions. No full neural covariance matrices or native ML dependencies are loaded.

The model trains on deliberate gentle placement (physical left/right) and explicit **More like this / Less like this** controls. Feedback applies once to the recent autonomous action, within 20 seconds of active companion time. Normal taps affect the expression but are not inferred to be likes/dislikes. Missing feedback is never a negative label. Throws, cancelled gestures and opening controls do not teach a preferred placement. Turning learning off suspends model use and updates without deleting its weights.

Session-local mood/attention orchestrates the learned choices and existing motion engine. Quick repeated taps can lead to guarded/annoyed eyes, flattened ears and a short retreat. Captured drags still follow the finger; roaming off and reduced motion prevent automatic retreat. Explicit gentle petting settles irritation, and touching/grabbing during departure still produces the previously specified happy rescue. Irritation and sadness decay with active time and absence; familiarity persists. This is expressive simulated affect, not a consciousness claim or a learned right to ignore controls.

An application-wide background actor owns training and atomic checkpoint writes. The CRC-checked, versioned checkpoint stores both networks, bounded experience and affect in private `noBackupFilesDir`, shared by main chat and floating assistant. It survives new conversations and app restarts, is excluded from Android backup, and records no transcript text, screenshots, message IDs or session identifiers. Decoding checks size, dimensions, enum bounds and finite numbers before applying a checkpoint. Reset increments an epoch so queued old-session callbacks cannot restore forgotten habits. Save failures keep the in-memory identity usable and appear in settings, with retry on a later interaction. Mood snapshots also flush on lifecycle stop/disposal; writes are coalesced with a 750 ms delay.

`CompanionMotionTest` has 38 cases covering motion, escape, gaze, departure/rescue, curiosity, guarded dragging, reduced-motion boundaries and the resting corner. `CompanionLearningTest` has 24 cases covering finite-difference gradients, real updates to both networks, signed residuals, learned action selection, checkpoint/replay limits, corruption, restart continuity, immutable policy snapshots, explicit feedback, teaching, mood decay and disabled learning. The thirteen `ChatCompanionTest` cases cover the existing chat/assistant regressions plus annoyed-state controls and retained/reset memory. The API 30 UI regression workflow runs them with the existing chat, keyboard and assistant tests.
